# Phase 3 report — Plan D (reduced form): individually fitted ABM policies → collective performance

Generated by `scripts/45_phase3_report.py` (2026-10-20). Pre-registered criteria: AGENT_SPEC §6.4. Reduced form decided by the human on 2026-10-20 after the pre-Phase-3 diagnostics: D-1 with per-participant ABM-policy parameters (no hypernetwork) plus a mean-preserving shuffle control; D-2 (neural policy) added by a later decision the same day (§6b). Teacher stays the empirical-Bayes fit (decision). Scripts: `40_phase3_fit_policy.py`, `41_phase3_simulate.py`, `42_phase3_observed_metrics.py`, `43_phase3_evaluate.py`, `44_phase3_extrapolate.py`, `50_env_validate.py`, `51_phase3_d2_train.py`, `52_phase3_d2_simulate.py`.

## 1. Execution environment

- CPU only (24 cores, 21 worker processes, single-threaded BLAS). Policy fits: ≈ 3 min. Leave-one-group-out simulation: **202,200 ABM runs in 1.1 h** (100 seeds for the point predictions, 50 shuffle draws × 10 seeds for each shuffle control). GPU unused (budget was 80 GPU-h).
- The paper's ABM (`external/analysis/agent_based_model/abm.py`, mesa 2.2.4) is used unmodified; it accepts per-agent lists for I, S, k, x0 and r_max.

## 2. Data and design

- 103 analysed groups (65 × 5, 38 × 4 members; 4-person groups simulated with 4 agents, as decided). Observed collective metrics from the paper's 1 Hz table (`observed_group_metrics.csv`): mean member gross score, tracking-time fraction, points per tracking second (pcr), member-level in/out-degree correlation, in-/out-degree means, median distance from the resource, discoverer in-degree gain (10 s after − 10 s before).
- **Individual policy = the paper's ABM decision rule, fitted to each participant's own rotations** (nothing else): tracking |Δθ| = I + S·det with probability 1 − p_soc, else |θ_pos| (turn to the visible others); searching Uniform(0, r_max) or |θ_pos|; p_soc = sigmoid(k (q − x0)) when someone is in the 90° FOV; Gaussian rotation noise σ; persistence π = P(no rotation). Likelihood: folded-normal mixture per second, zero-rotation seconds as a point mass. MAP with a Gaussian prior centred on the pooled fit of the participant's condition × speed cell **estimated on the other folds** (leave-one-group-out respected; no group outcome enters anywhere, §6.2). Detector-scale conversion data → ABM: S_abm = S·(1 + v).
- Model-level ABM parameters (one value per simulation): noise σ = 10° (paper value; the fitted σ ≈ 40° absorbs purposeful large turns as noise and destroys tracking in the ABM — smoke test), persistence = group mean of the fitted π, p_s / p_u = paper values.

## 3. Deviations from defaults

- D-1 hypernetwork replaced by direct per-participant MAP fits of the ABM policy (the "z" is the 5-vector I, S, k, x0, r_max plus π). D-2 implemented as a participant-embedding policy (z fitted per held-out participant with a frozen fold network) rather than Phase 1's decoder (b), because the Phase 1 search-phase encoder was abandoned. Rationale: Phase 1 showed non-linear z unnecessary; the diagnostics showed the limiting factor is measurement, not policy fidelity.
- Added controls: ctrl1b (all parameters pooled per cell), ctrl2b (the group's own mean agent — isolates composition), ctrl4b (mean-preserving shuffle). Control 3 (nominal group) implemented as each member simulated alone with their own policy, averaged.
- Budget reductions (§6.5 order): seeds 200 → 100; shuffle 100 → 50 draws × 10 seeds.
- Primary correlation is pooled over groups after z-scoring within cell (controls that are constant within a cell have r ≈ 0 by construction; raw pooled r and within-speed r are also reported).

## 5. Pass/fail (pre-registered §6.4)

- **D-H1** (score and pcr: Δr ≥ 0.10 and p < 0.01 vs control 1 *and* control 2): score vs paper ABM Δr = 0.650 (p = 0.000), vs average agent Δr = 0.927 (p = 0.000); pcr vs paper ABM Δr = 0.515 (p = 0.000), vs average agent Δr = 0.735 (p = 0.000) → **PASS**.
- **D-H2** (re-composition shuffle, permutation p < 0.01 for score and pcr): random shuffle p = 0.000 (score), 0.000 (pcr) → **PASS**; mean-preserving shuffle p = 0.012 (score), 0.193 (pcr) → **FAIL**.
- Power caveat (pre-stated): 103 groups, 52 slow / 51 fast; most between-group signal is in the slow condition.
- **Substantive D-H2 test (human decision 2026-10-20): the group-mean-agent control.** Δr(main − group-mean agents) = +0.013 [-0.03, 0.06] (score), +0.023 (pcr), +0.011 (tracking time) → **no composition effect**. The pre-registered shuffle results above are reported as written.

### Reading

1. **Individually fitted policies predict which groups perform well.** Leave-one-group-out, with nothing fitted to group outcomes, the ABM reproduces the across-group ranking of score (r = 0.55 within cell; raw r 0.75), tracking time (0.54), efficiency (0.45) and median distance (0.41). The prediction holds within group-size strata (score r 0.56 / 0.75 for 4- / 5-person slow groups, 0.72 / 0.55 fast), so it is not a group-size artefact. The pre-registered controls 1 and 2 are constant within a cell and therefore carry r ≈ 0: **D-H1 passes, but through the information in the members' parameters, not through anything specific to heterogeneity.**
2. **No composition effect.** The decisive comparison is the group's own mean agent (ctrl2b: every member replaced by the group-mean policy): Δr(main − ctrl2b) = +0.013 [-0.03, 0.06] for score, +0.023 for pcr. Removing all within-group spread loses nothing. The composition regression agrees (SD block p = 0.46 for score). The pre-registered random shuffle "passes" only because it changes the group mean (null r ≈ 0); the mean-preserving shuffle leaves a residual gap (null r ≈ 0.43 vs 0.55, p ≈ 0.01) that is attributable to imperfect mean matching (the 5 % closest of 1,000 random sets) rather than to composition, given the exact mean-agent result. **D-H2 is therefore not supported in substance**, consistent with Phase 2 and the pre-Phase-3 diagnostics.
3. **Interaction matters a little.** The nominal-group control (members simulated alone) predicts slightly worse than the full group simulation (Δr = +0.101, p = 0.001 for score; tracking time Δr +0.092): in the ABM, putting the fitted individuals together adds some information beyond their solitary performance, but it is the mean of their traits that carries it.
4. **Network structure is not predicted.** In-degree, in/out-degree asymmetry and the discoverer in-degree gain have r ≈ 0 (the out-degree r ≈ 0.48 is a group-size artefact: 4 vs 5 agents). The ABM's visibility dynamics do not reproduce the observed role structure at the group level; Phase 2 showed that roles are predictable from individual movement style, so this is a limitation of the ABM's social rule rather than of the individual measurements.
5. Which parameters carry the prediction: the reliable ones (persistence π, r = 0.84; selectivity k, 0.70), i.e. how much a person rotates at all and how selectively they use social information. Both act through the group mean.

## 6b. D-2: z-conditioned neural policy (decision 2026-10-20)

Environment: the paper's ABM re-implemented in vectorised numpy (`src/collective_id/env.py`) so that arbitrary policies can be simulated; validated on the Fig 5B grid against the mesa ABM (tracking time r = 0.997, mean |Δ| = 0.019; pcr r = 0.996, mean |Δ| = 0.44 points/s; the qualitative Fig 5B pattern is reproduced). Policy: shared MLP (2 × 64) mapping the paper's per-second features (tracking flag, detector, detector change, distance, with-social flag, #visible, signed social angle, social movement direction, social quality) plus the previous second's rotation state, and a 4-d participant embedding z, to P(no rotation), a truncated-Exponential magnitude and a direction probability. Trained per fold on the training participants' rotations only (412–427 k seconds); held-out participants' z fitted on their own data with the network frozen (leave-one-group-out). Individual-level check (fit z on the first half, evaluate the second half, all seconds incl. zero mass): individual z improves held-out NLL over the population policy by 0.078 nats/s (85 % of participants; tracking seconds 0.090) — unlike the ABM-policy parameters of D-1, the learned z does generalise within person. Simulation: 88,900 runs in 4 min (100 seeds per group and variant).

## 7. Anomalies / failures

- Fitting the ABM policy to human data needed two fixes before it was stable: the folded likelihood is invariant to (I, S) → (−I, −S) (I constrained ≥ 0), and exact zero-rotation seconds drove σ → 0 unless treated as a point mass. The fitted σ (≈ 40°) is far above the paper's 10°: human rotation magnitudes while moving are much more variable than the ABM's deterministic rule + small noise; using the fitted σ in the ABM makes agents unable to track (smoke test), so the paper value is used and this is a stated limitation of the ABM as a model of humans.
- The ABM under-predicts absolute tracking time (e.g. slow FP groups ≈ 0.6–0.7 simulated vs ≈ 0.9 observed); only relative (across-group) prediction is evaluated.
- The first mean-preserving shuffle matched the five per-agent parameters but not persistence (model-level, group mean); it was re-run with persistence included (`sim_runs_mps_v1_no_persistence_match.parquet` keeps the first version: its null r was 0.38, i.e. persistence carries much of the prediction).
- Extrapolation note: with human-calibrated policies (π ≈ 0.3, r_max ≈ 85°, I ≈ 35°) tracking times are far below the paper's stylised agents at every speed and group size; heterogeneous samples track somewhat *longer* than homogeneous mean agents at slow speeds (0.2–0.4), the opposite sign to the pre-Phase-3 diagnostic that used the exp-mapped parameters: this landscape is model-dependent and unvalidated.
- Oversubscribed BLAS threads made the first fitting attempt 10× slower; fixed with single-threaded workers.

## 8. Unverified

- Whether any policy class could reproduce the observed role structure (in/out asymmetry, discoverer attention); neither the ABM rule nor the neural policy does.
- The 35 excluded fifth members of 4-person groups influenced the real groups but are not simulated.
