# Wu et al. (2025) replication — Collective-ID analyses on a second dataset (2026-10-20)

Tables: `reports/wu2025_replication_tables.md` (generated by `scripts/68_wu_report.py`). Figures: `results/wu2025/fig_*.png|pdf`. Decision log: `reports/questions.md` (2026-10-20, ext).

## 0. Why this dataset

Wu, Deffner, Kahl, Meder, Ho & Kurvers (2025, *Nat Commun* 16:3539) ran a collective foraging task in Minecraft with the same visual-field transcription machinery as Chirkov et al. (2026): 128 participants in 32 fixed groups of 4; 16 rounds of 120 s per participant, 8 solo and 8 group, half in "random" and half in "smooth" reward environments, in counterbalanced blocks of 4; 20 Hz positions and head direction; per-second visibility of every other player (Unity transcription); reward-splash observations. The decisive design feature relative to Chirkov is the **within-subject solo condition**: an individual representation z can be measured with no social input and then tested on the group rounds of the same person, and every statistic has eight repetitions for reliability.

## 1. Data and teacher

- Raw logs (`external/wu2025/analysis/data/2021batch`) parsed to `data/processed/wu2025/{rounds,players,blocks,evis}.parquet` (`scripts/61`); round index k ↔ raw file `ge_{k+2}` (two training rounds), verified against the authors' block cache.
- Teacher = the authors' block-choice model (ARS + conditional social learning; features per candidate block: distance, in-FOV, GP reward prediction, distance to successful visible players, distance to other visible players; adaptive terms × time since last own reward). Per-participant deviations were re-fitted by MAP (batched L-BFGS, `scripts/62`) with the authors' population posterior as prior, on all group rounds and on halves. Validation against the authors' Stan posterior means (`v_ID`): r = 0.99 (Locality), 0.95 (BlockVisibility), 0.97 (RewardPrediction), 0.95 (SuccessfulProximity), 0.93 (UnsuccessfulProximity), 0.86–0.99 (adaptive terms); solo model 0.84–0.997.

## 2. Noise ceilings (split-half, rounds 1–2 vs 3–4 of each block)

| quantity | r split-half | Chirkov analogue |
|---|---|---|
| choice weights, group rounds: Locality / BlockVisibility | 0.83 / 0.76 | Intercept 0.76, Detector 0.60 |
| RewardPrediction | 0.43 | — |
| SuccessfulProximity / UnsuccessfulProximity | 0.45 / 0.27 | SocPos 0.30, SocMov 0.17 |
| adaptive social terms | 0.16–0.20 | quality interactions 0.09–0.12 |
| solo movement statistics (speed, coverage, turning, inter-block distance, ARS index) | 0.70–0.97 | — |
| group-round visibility: out-degree (others in own view) / in-degree (self in others' view) | 0.59 / 0.24 | — |
| group-round reward per person | 0.56 | — |

Same ordering as in Chirkov: private processing (how far one goes, whether one targets blocks in view, reward prediction) is reliably individual; the weight given to successful others is moderately reliable (0.45, better than Chirkov's 0.30, with 8 × 2 min of group play); the weight given to unsuccessful others and the adaptive social terms are at the measurement floor. The same movement statistics are also highly consistent between solo and group rounds of the same person (r 0.63–0.94 for 19 of 21 statistics), i.e. movement style is a trait that survives the presence of others.

## 3. Solo z

PCA (4 components, 33/19/11/10 % of variance) of 21 standardised solo statistics, fitted within training folds (8 folds × 4 sessions). z0 = mobility/throughput (path length, speed, cells covered, blocks opened); z1 = long inter-block hops and slow looking (exploratory step length); z2 = turning and looking jitter, weak area-restricted search; z3 = solo success (rewards, reward rate).

## 4. Transfer solo → group (Phase 1 / Phase 2 analogues; out-of-fold r over 128 participants; Δr vs the solo-reward baseline with session-cluster bootstrap)

- **Private choice weights in group rounds are predicted from solo behaviour**: Locality r = 0.64 (z alone) / 0.80 (z + solo choice weights), ceiling 0.83; BlockVisibility 0.66 / 0.65, ceiling 0.76; RewardPrediction 0.25 / 0.51, ceiling 0.43. Δr vs solo reward 0.45–1.04, all p < 0.001.
- **Social weights are not predicted**: SuccessfulProximity r = −0.23 / −0.17 (ceiling 0.45), UnsuccessfulProximity −0.24 / −0.28 (ceiling 0.27), adaptive social terms ≤ 0.01 except AdaptSuccessful with z + choice weights (0.01, Δr 0.26, p 0.002 — against a baseline that is itself negative). Negative out-of-fold r means "no signal" (held-out-fold mean artefact). Note that SuccessfulProximity has reliable individual variance (0.45) that solo behaviour simply does not carry: payoff-selective social learning is a disposition that is only expressed, and only measurable, in the social context.
- **Visibility roles**: out-degree (looking at others) r = 0.25 / 0.40 (ceiling 0.59), Δr 0.41 / 0.56, p < 0.001; in-degree (being looked at) r ≈ 0 (ceiling 0.24). Inter-individual distance (nearest-neighbour, centroid, fraction of time within 10 blocks) r 0.26–0.34 (ceilings 0.36–0.50). Fraction of others' reward splashes seen 0.31 / 0.35 (ceiling 0.37, i.e. at ceiling).
- **Group-round individual reward**: z r = 0.57, z + choice 0.62, solo reward alone 0.61 (ceiling 0.56): performance transfers, and z adds nothing beyond solo performance (Δr −0.03 / +0.01).
- Compared with Chirkov Phase 2 (early 3 min → remaining 12 min): there, in-degree was predictable (Δr 0.11) and out-degree not; here, out-degree is predictable and in-degree is not. In-degree here has a split-half reliability of only 0.24 (2-minute rounds, 3 potential observers), so this is a ceiling difference, not a contradiction; the decomposition below gives the consistent picture.

## 5. Decomposition of group-round individual outcomes (1024 participant-rounds; env + own solo reward → + own z → + others' mean z → + group SD z)

| outcome | + own z | + others' mean z | + group SD z |
|---|---|---|---|
| out-degree | p < 0.001 (R²m 0.002 → 0.054) | p 0.69 | p 0.76 |
| in-degree | p 0.27 | **p 0.001** (0.015 → 0.034) | p 0.26 |
| reward | p 0.001 | p 0.13 | p 0.88 |
| nearest-neighbour distance | p 0.004 | p 0.79 | p 0.44 |
| splashes seen, time near others | n.s. (LRT degenerate) | n.s. | n.s. |

Looking at others is one's own trait; being looked at depends on who one is with (others' mean z); the within-group spread of z adds nothing to any outcome. This is the Phase 2 result (own z ≈ 10 %, others' mean 5–8 %, SD ≈ 0) reproduced with an uncontaminated solo z. Effect sizes are smaller (marginal R² ≤ 0.06 for roles) because single 2-minute rounds are noisier than a 12-minute session.

## 6. Group outcomes and composition (256 group rounds; outcomes z-scored within env)

- **Zero-shot**: the sum of the members' mean solo reward in the same environment predicts the group's reward with r = 0.39 [0.25, 0.52] per round and 0.55 when the four rounds of a session × env are averaged (64 cells). This needs no fitting at all and is the analogue of Phase 3 control 3 (nominal group).
- Leave-one-session-out ridge on member means (z4 + solo reward) does not beat the single mean-solo-reward predictor (Δr −0.01 [−0.09, 0.08]). Adding the within-group SDs of z and of solo reward **lowers** out-of-sample r (Δr −0.11 [−0.18, −0.04]); in-sample the SD block adds ΔR² = 0.008 (permutation p 0.77). SD-only models have r < 0.
- Collective gain (group reward − nominal expectation) is unrelated to any SD (Spearman ρ 0.01–0.06, p ≥ 0.33) and negatively related to the members' mean solo reward (ρ −0.32, p < 0.001: good solitary foragers gain less from the group, a ceiling/regression effect).
- Other group-level outcomes: visibility density (mean out-degree) r 0.18–0.20 from member means, SD adds nothing (p 0.64–0.96); nearest-neighbour distance r ≈ 0.1, SD block ΔR² 0.04 (p 0.034); spread of rewards within the group (reward SD) r ≈ 0, SD block ΔR² 0.04 (p 0.049); asymmetry of in-degree r −0.25, SD block Δr +0.16 (p 0.001, but from a negative base). Across 7 outcomes × 2 tests, two spatial-structure tests reach p < 0.05 at ΔR² ≈ 0.04 — consistent with the Chirkov diagnostic (observed SD increment ΔR² 0.02–0.04, p 0.19–0.35) and not with a performance-relevant composition effect.
